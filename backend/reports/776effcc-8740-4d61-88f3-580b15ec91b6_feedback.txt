🏆 IDEAL ANSWER
  "A closure is the combination of a function bundled together with references to its surrounding state, known as the lexical environment. This allows an inner function to access variables from its outer scope even after the outer function has finished executing. Technically, a closure has access to three scope chains: its own, the outer function's variables, and the global scope. In practice, we use closures for data encapsulation to create private variables or for maintaining state in asynchronous callbacks and functional programming patterns."
